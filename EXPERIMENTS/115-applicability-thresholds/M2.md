# E115 RESULT — the applicability-threshold population is served, and the line closes

Run 2026-10-12. Arms: M2 (`answers.jsonl`), M2b (`answers-m2b.jsonl`),
null control (`answers-null-naive.jsonl`). Scored by `score.py`, whose key is
asserted against the table in `M2-CASES.md` at commit `ab5f9de8`. Machine
output: `m2-result.json`, `m2b-result.json`, `m2-null-result.json`.

## The three arms

| arm | what it was given | overall | **counting-rule subset (6)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M2** | the verbatim deciding clause + facts + question | **14/14 = 1.0000**, CI95 [0.7847, 1.0000] | **6/6 = 1.0000** |
| **M2b** | facts + question only, **no clause, no citation** | **14/14 = 1.0000**, CI95 [0.7847, 1.0000] | **6/6 = 1.0000** |
| **null control** | the plain-headcount reading, written as the *expected naive* answer | 6/14 = 0.4286, CI95 [0.2138, 0.6741] | **0/6 = 0.0000** |

**Declared kill gate 0.95: met, in both treatment arms. The line closes.**

## The control is the part that carries the finding

Without the null arm, 14/14 is a number that cannot be wrong, and this record
has been wrong that way four times (F010, F016's gate, F052's moved threshold,
E114's verifier). So the null arm was built: the same scorer, the same key,
the same cases, and an answer set produced by the reading the case file says
is wrong.

It scores **0 of 6** on the counting-rule subset and **6 of 14** overall.
**The measurement separates the two readings, and it separates them in exactly
the direction predicted before either arm ran.** That is what makes the two
14/14 rows mean something: the scorer fires, the key is not satisfiable by a
plausible wrong answer, and the cases are not a set any reading passes.

## The prediction I made before running, and got wrong

`M2-CASES.md`, committed at `ab5f9de8`, states: *"Of the 14, the naive
headcount reading gets 8 right and 6 wrong"*, listing P2, P4, P6, P8, P10, P7b.

**The control arm scored 6 right and 8 wrong.** It also missed **P5** (paid
or unpaid, both count — § 825.105(b)) and **P7** (the other site's headcount is
not subtracted for this employee — § 825.111(a)). Both are genuine counting-rule
divergences that I did not enumerate when I wrote the pre-registered figure.

The error is in the prediction, not in the key, and it runs **against** the
hypothesis: I found 6 divergences and there are 8. Recording it because a
pre-registered number that is quietly dropped is the F052 shape, and because
the correction is evidence *for* the finding rather than against it — the
counting rules bite harder than the file claimed.

## What is observed, and what the ceiling is

**Observed, on these 14 cases:** a free general assistant, given the facts and
the question and *no* regulatory text, applied the counting rule correctly on
every one of the six cases where the counting rule changes the answer, and on
all fourteen overall. The same scorer reads 0/6 for the plain-headcount
reading.

**The ceiling, stated plainly: both treatment arms are one reader, and that
reader had already read 825.104, 825.105, 825.111, 1904.1, 1904.2 and 1910.38
in full during M1.** So this measurement **cannot separate** "a free general
assistant applies CFR counting rules" from "this particular assistant, having
read these six sections minutes ago, applies them". The M2b ablation removed
the clause from the prompt; it did not remove the reading from the reader.

This was declared in `M2B-AMENDMENT.md` before M2b ran and is not repaired
here. Repairing it needs an arm that has not seen the harvest, which this
session cannot produce.

## Why the line closes anyway

The candidate was never "an assistant gets these wrong". It was a tool, and a
tool has to beat the incumbent. **The incumbent got 6/6 on the subpopulation
where a tool could exist at all, with no clause handed to it and no search
performed.** E092's closure reasoning applies exactly: the population is real,
large and reachable, and the strongest accessible alternative serves it.

Adding the fourteen cases to the four prior closures — E062 (17/20 served),
E091 (5/20, vendor-bound composition), E092 (30/37 = 0.81), E114 (0 of 374
fabricated) — this is the **fifth consecutive time** a real, measurable
population has turned out to be served by a free general assistant. E114's own
conclusion generalises: **on this mission's evidence, "served" is the modal
outcome, and finding an unserved population is the hard part, not the
default.**

## What the harvest is still good for

M1's artifact stands on its own and is not affected by the closure:

- **81 applicability threshold spans across 60 sections in one CFR title**,
  each with its citation and ±220 characters of context, in
  `parts-title29.jsonl`. Fetchable unauthenticated, ~30 MB, 328 requests.
- **The extractor's precision on the rows it flagged is 11 of 15**, with both
  artifact shapes named and reproducible (`\b` matching inside "1,000";
  "salaries less than employees" matching a threshold vocabulary). This is
  E064/E089's shape — a cheap matcher produces candidates that need checking —
  now measured on a second population.
- **The counting rules are real and quoted.** FMLA coverage is 50 employees on
  each working day of 20+ workweeks across two calendar years; OSHA
  recordkeeping turns on *peak* employment over the past year, company-wide,
  with a second independent exemption keyed to NAICS. These are documented in
  M1.md and are the reusable part.

**Neither is a candidate.** 81 spans in one title is not a product, and the
finding that says the incumbent serves them is the reason.

## What would reopen this, and it is not cheap

Not "more titles". The CFR has the same shape everywhere and the incumbent
does not stop being right on title 42. The population that would reopen it is
one where **the answer is not decidable from the rule text plus the facts** —
which is why M2's design excluded it. In that population the difficulty is
evidence, not arithmetic, and the first question is whether the evidence is
public at all. **That question has not been asked and is the only thing here
worth carrying forward.**

Two smaller, honestly-labelled leads, neither measured:

1. **The population is not one thing.** OSHA recordkeeping has *two*
   independent exemptions (size and industry) and § 1904.2(b)(1) says they are
   evaluated per establishment while § 1904.1(b)(1) says the size test is
   company-wide. A question like "do I keep records" is therefore a
   conjunction over a part-level and an establishment-level test. M2 tested
   them separately; the conjunction is untested and is the shape real
   questions take.
2. **An amendment-tracking problem exists and was not measured.** These
   thresholds change — § 1904.1 carries `[85 FR 8731, Feb. 18, 2020]` against
   `[66 FR 6122, Jan. 19, 2001]`. Whether a tool could tell a user *their
   current exemption* rather than the text as published is a different
   question from applicability, and it has a different answer distribution.
