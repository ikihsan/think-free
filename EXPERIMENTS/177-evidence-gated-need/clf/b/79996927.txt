=== TITLE ===
Should (double)(std::float16_t(1024) + std::float16_t(1025)) be 2048.0 or 2049.0?

=== QUESTION ===
I'm running into some behavior of std::float16_t (and std::bfloat16_t ) that I don't under
stand. 
 My understanding and expectation is that: 
 
 the next std::float16_t after 1024 is 1025 . 
 1024+1025=2049 isn't representable as std::float16_t , so it gets rounded-to-even to 2048
 . 
 therefore, (std::float16_t(1024) + std::float16_t(1025)) evaluates to std::float16_t(2048
) . 
 if I then convert that result to float or double , I should get 2048.0f or 2048.0 , respe
ctively. 
 
 So I expect that: 
 
 (float)(std::float16_t(1024) + std::float16_t(1025)) should be 2048.0f . 
 (double)(std::float16_t(1024) + std::float16_t(1025)) should be 2048.0 . 
 
 But, they come out 2049.0f and 2049.0 instead. 
 Why is this? 
 Similarly, using std::bfloat16_t instead of std::float16_t , I expect that: 
 
 (float)(std::bfloat16_t(128) + std::bfloat16_t(129)) should be 256.0f . 
 (double)(std::bfloat16_t(128) + std::bfloat16_t(129)) should be 256.0 . 
 
 But, they come out 257.0f and 257.0 instead. 
 I observed this using g++ 15.2.0. 
 The following C++ program demonstrates this issue:

=== KEY ANSWER ===
It can be either value. An implementation is permitted but not required to represent inter
mediate expression results with wider precision. ( [expr.pre]/6 ) 
 
 The values of the floating-point operands and the results of floating-point expressions m
ay be represented in greater precision and range than that required by the type; the types
 are not changed thereby. 
 
 For GCC, there's the -fexcess-precision= option to control whether to use a wider precisi
on.
