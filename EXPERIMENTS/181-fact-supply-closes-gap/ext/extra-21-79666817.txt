TITLE: What is the progress on safety for C++?
TAGS: c++,memory-safety
QUESTION AS POSTED:
I have been reading up on safety profiles , safe C++ and some other articles on the matter ( example ). I would like one of these features to be adopted, and the only one that I believe has actually been prototyped is the safe c++ proposal . There also seems to be some strongly worded papers talking about all the problems with profiles ( example ), which I believe has now been voted as the way forward for investment. 
 What is the current state of affairs with safe C++ making its way to the standard? Could users hope for C++26? C++29? Also, is there a list of checks/safety features that may get merged? This paper seems to claim C++26 may be on the agenda, but I'm not sure whether this is still on the table.

- [Peter] @Kaz - I believe that's part of Fareanor's point. A lot of the claims about lack of safety in C++ come about because people use its features without taking care to use it safely. There is often a trade-off between "safety" (hand-holding the programmer to catch/prevent unsafe usage) and "expressive power" (being able to write code to do useful things, but relying on programmer discipline and skill to achieve safe usage). Both aspects are important, but safety is often being advocated now in a way that reduces ability to apply skill and expressive power to address real-world problems.
