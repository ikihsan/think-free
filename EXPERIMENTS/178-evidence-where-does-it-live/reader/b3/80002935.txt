=== TITLE ===
Why is there no std::bin stream manipulator in C++?

=== QUESTION ===
I am aware that there are three I/O manipulators (namely std::hex , std::oct , and std::de
c ) provided by the standard C++. But I've always wondered why there is no std::bin manipu
lator in the standard library. 
 Note that I am not looking for workarounds, but instead want to know the historical reaso
n(s) why no std::bin was added to the language. I am also aware of std::bitset , etc, but 
I want to know the reasons why it was not added to the standard, which may be documented s
omewhere in the old C++ docs, etc. 
 I think there can be some length ambiguity in printing in this form, but there may be mor
e reasons.

=== KEY ANSWER (highest scored) ===
<iostream> was originally modeled after the legacy C printf API lacking support for binary
 format. It was meant to make a safe replacement for variadic functions. 
 It wasn't until C++14 that the 0b prefix was adopted into C++ as the binary literal value
 indicator, and by that time, the critical design flaws of <iostream> were well-known. Bes
ides that the C++11 adoption of variadic templates had already started the quest for simpl
er interfaces.
Today <iostream> is maintained as legacy burden. 
 The output is now carried out using C++23 header <print> with std::print , and std::print
ln handling standard output as well as formatted file output to std::ostream , std::ofstre
am , and the C API std::FILE * . The new std::formatter adds support for binary through b 
format argument. 
 On a C++20 setup <format> gives std::format — using same std::formatter — as the replacem
ent for sprintf . So combination of std::puts , or std::fputs with std::format and std::st
ring::c_str can temporarily fill in the place of <print> . 
 Otherwise the refrence implementation of <format> and <print> is the {fmt} library — comp
atible with C++17 . 
 All in all, <iostream> is only being used for istream input, in modern code. If {scanlib}
 hits the maturity level of {fmt} , or a better implementation of C++ regexp emerges, we c
an finally totally retire <iostream> as deprecated. So, short answer to original question 
is that <iostream> is not worth more investment.
