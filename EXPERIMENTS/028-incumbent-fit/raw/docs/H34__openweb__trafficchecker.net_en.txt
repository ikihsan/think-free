 What a traffic check returns 
 A website traffic checker estimates how many people reach a domain in a typical month and how that traffic is split. Traffic Checker pulls a snapshot for the domain you submit: monthly visits, recent visit history, search versus direct versus social versus referral versus mail, bounce rate, pages per visit, time on site, global rank, country mix, and the keywords tied to the estimate. 
 The same snapshot can include domain authority, page rank, spam score, and WHOIS fields (age, registrar, nameservers, expiry). Those are separate entities from visits. Use them together when you compare two sites, not as substitutes for each other. 
 Results are third-party estimates, not the site owner&#039;s analytics and not server logs. They are useful for ranking domains against each other, spotting a dead property, or checking whether a niche still draws search demand. 
 
 
 Monthly visits and traffic history 
 Monthly visits is the headline number most people want from a site traffic checker. It is an estimate of sessions in the latest available month, not unique users and not revenue. Treat large round numbers as order of magnitude. A site at 8,000 visits and one at 12,000 are in the same band. A site at 80,000 is a different band. 
 Where history is present, you see how that estimate moved month to month. A steady line matters more than a single spike. Spikes without a matching keyword or source shift are often noise. A slow decline across several months is a real signal if you are buying a domain, placing ads, or using the site as a backlink target. 
 Small or new sites may return thin or empty visit data. That is a result, not a bug. If you need authority or registration age anyway, run the domain authority checker or the domain age checker on the same hostname. 
 
 
 Traffic sources: search, direct, social, referral, mail 
 Source mix answers how people arrive. Search share is the portion attributed to organic or paid search. Direct is typed-in or bookmarked traffic and also absorbs some unassigned visits. Social, referral, and mail are the remaining channels in the snapshot. 
 A content site that ranks should show a meaningful search share. A brand or app destination often shows more direct. A site that lives on guest posts and directories shows referral. None of those mixes is "good" on its own. Match the mix to the job: outreach wants search plus topical keywords; a sponsorship wants enough total visits and a country mix that matches the campaign; a domain purchase wants visits that did not appear yesterday and vanish tomorrow. 
 If search share is high but the keyword list is empty or off-topic, do not trust the story yet. Keywords and sources should agree. If they do not, treat the estimate as weaker. 
 
 
 Bounce rate, pages per visit, and time on site 
 Engagement metrics describe what happens after the click. Bounce rate is the share of visits that leave without another recorded pageview. Pages per visit is average depth. Time on site is average duration. High bounce with high pages per visit on the same domain is inconsistent; prefer the rest of the snapshot over a single contradictory field. 
 A tool, login, or documentation site can bounce a lot and still be useful. A magazine or blog that bounces immediately and has almost no time on site is a weak content property. Use these numbers to compare similar site types, not a SaaS app against a news homepage. 
 
 
 Countries, keywords, and rank 
 Top countries show where the estimated audience sits. That matters for language, ads, and whether a "US blog" is actually read in markets you do not sell to. Global rank is a relative position among sites in the same dataset. It moves with the estimate; it is not a ranking for a search query. 
 Top keywords are the queries the model associates with the domain. Use them to see topic, not to copy a list into your own site. Volume and CPC, when shown, describe the query market, not guaranteed traffic you will receive if you write those terms. 
 For link building, a domain whose keywords match your page topic is more useful than a higher visit count on an unrelated niche. For competitive research, compare keyword overlap between two hostnames in the same category. 
 
 
 Estimates, refresh, and what this tool is not 
 Providers sample panels, crawls, and clickstream. Coverage is better on sites that already receive a lot of visits. Brand-new domains, private apps, and sites behind logins often look empty. Cache on our side means a domain you checked recently may not refetch every field on the next request. 
 This is not live server traffic, not a search-engine webmaster report, and not a login to another analytics product. Domain authority and spam score come from link-index models. WHOIS comes from registration records. AI referral figures, when present, are a separate slice of the same snapshot and have their own tool page. 
 Free checks are for one domain at a time in the browser. Credits cover bulk CSV and API lookups. Credits do not expire. See pricing if you are processing lists rather than a handful of URLs. 
 
 

 
 Questions about checking website traffic 
 
 
 
 Is the website traffic checker free? 
 
 
 
 
 
 Yes. One domain at a time on this page does not require an account. Bulk CSV uploads and API calls use credits. 
 
 
 
 
 How accurate are monthly visit estimates? 
 
 
 
 
 
 They are third-party estimates. Use them to compare domains and see order of magnitude. Do not treat them as the site owner&#039;s analytics. 
 
 
 
 
 Can I check any domain? 
 
 
 
 
 
 You can submit a public hostname. Enter example.com or a full URL. We strip the path and check the registered domain. IP addresses are not accepted. 
 
 
 
 
 What is the difference between this page and the other tools? 
 
 
 
 
 
 This page is the full traffic snapshot. The authority, age, and AI tools highlight those parts of the same family of data so you can land on the entity you care about from search.
